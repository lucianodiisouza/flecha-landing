Privacy Policy

Last updated: 25 October 2026

The short version

  • The Flecha app does not collect, send or sell any information about you. There is no account. The only Flecha server hands out downloads and updates.
  • Your tabs, Spaces, history, settings and Boosts are files on your Mac.
  • When you browse, you talk to the websites you visit and to the services you choose, under their own policies.
  • This website counts visits with Google Analytics, which sets cookies. Section 3 says what it collects and how to turn it off. The app itself never talks to Google Analytics.

1. Who we are

Flecha is an open-source web browser for macOS, maintained by OPrimoDev. In this policy, “Flecha”, “we” and “us” mean the project and its maintainer. The source code is public at github.com/lucianodiisouza/flecha-browser.

2. The Flecha app

What we collect

Nothing. The app has no analytics, no telemetry, no crash reporting and no account. It does not send your browsing history, your tabs or any identifier to us. Our only servers hand out downloads and updates, and the app sends them nothing about you.

What stays on your Mac

Flecha keeps its data in your Flecha profile folder, ~/Library/Application Support/Flecha Browser: tabs, Spaces, favorites, history, downloads list, settings, keyboard shortcuts, cookies and site data, and your Boosts. You can back up, move or delete these files at any time. Deleting them, or uninstalling Flecha, removes the data.

Connections the app makes

Like any browser, Flecha connects to the internet on your behalf. Apart from update checks, these connections go to third parties, not to us:

  • Websites you visit, and the resources they load.
  • Your search engine, when you search from the command bar. You choose it in Settings.
  • Site icons, which Flecha fetches from the sites in your sidebar.
  • The Chrome Web Store and extension updates, for extensions you install. Each extension has its own privacy policy.
  • Blocker list updates for the built-in ad and tracker blocker.
  • Services built into Chromium, such as Safe Browsing or component updates, according to Chromium's defaults and your settings.
  • Google or other accounts, only if you sign in to them. Those providers' policies apply.
  • Updates from flecha.dev. Flecha uses Sparkle to check flecha.dev/appcast.xml for new versions on its own schedule, and when you choose Check for Updates… in the app menu. Updates download from dl.flecha.dev. These requests carry what any web request carries, such as your IP address and the app's version. Flecha adds nothing about you, and Sparkle's anonymous system profile is off. Our hosting providers, Vercel and Cloudflare, may keep standard logs of these requests.

Importing from other browsers

When you import, Flecha reads bookmarks, history and, for Arc, your sidebar from files on your Mac. The data is copied into your Flecha profile and never leaves your computer.

Coding agents (MCP)

The MCP bridge is off by default. When you turn it on, it listens only on 127.0.0.1 and requires a token. Agents you connect, such as Claude Code or Cursor, can read and act on your tabs. What those agents do with that information, including sending it to their AI providers, is governed by those tools and providers. Only connect agents you trust, and turn the bridge off when you don't need it.

Boosts and extensions

Boosts are code you write or install. Extensions are made by third parties. Both run in pages you visit and can read them. Review what you install.

3. This website

flecha.dev is a static website, and so are its docs at flecha.dev/docs. We do not ask for any personal information through it. The only cookies it sets are Google Analytics’ own, described below.

  • Hosting. The site runs on Vercel, and downloads come from Cloudflare (dl.flecha.dev). Both may keep standard server logs, such as IP address, browser and the page or file requested, for security and operations.
  • Fonts. The site loads its typefaces from Google Fonts, so your browser connects to Google when you open it.
  • Analytics. We use Google Analytics 4 to see, in aggregate, how many people visit, which pages they read, where they came from (such as a search engine or a link) and how many click a download. It sets first-party cookies (_ga and _ga_*) with a random ID, and Google receives your IP address, browser, device type, approximate location and the pages you view. Google Analytics 4 does not log or store IP addresses. We have turned off Google signals and ad personalisation, we don’t link this data to advertising, and we never sell it. Google keeps it for up to 14 months under Google’s privacy policy. To opt out, block googletagmanager.com with a content blocker (Flecha’s built-in blocker does this), or install Google’s opt-out add-on.
  • Links. Links to GitHub and other sites take you to services with their own policies.

4. Children

Flecha is a general-purpose tool and is not directed to children. Since we collect no personal data, we hold none about children.

5. Your rights

Depending on where you live, laws such as Brazil's LGPD or the EU's GDPR give you rights to access, correct, delete and port your personal data, and to object to its processing. Because the app keeps your data only on your Mac, you exercise most of these rights directly by managing your Flecha profile. For anything related to this website, contact us as described below.

6. Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, we will update the date at the top of the page. The full history of changes is public in the website's repository.

7. Contact

For privacy questions or requests, open an issue at github.com/lucianodiisouza/flecha-browser/issues. Please don't include personal data in a public issue; ask for a private channel instead.
